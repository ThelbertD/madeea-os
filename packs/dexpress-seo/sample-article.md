## What "rekey" actually means

Rekeying changes the working key of a lock without swapping out the whole lock hardware. A locksmith opens the lock cylinder, removes the small spring-loaded pins inside, and replaces them with a new set matched to a brand-new key. The moment that is done, every old key stops working. The lock on your door looks identical, feels identical, and functions identically — but the keys the previous owner kept are now useless metal.

Think of it like changing the password on an email account. You keep the same inbox; you just lock out anyone who knew the old login. Rekeying does the same thing for your front door, back door, garage entry, and deadbolts.

Here is a practical tip for new Ambler homeowners: walk the entire property before the locksmith arrives and count every keyed entry point. Front door, back slider, side garage door, basement bulkhead, and any detached shed or gate. List them out. That count determines your job scope and helps us bring the right hardware to your driveway the first time.

## Rekey vs. replace: which one do you need?

Most people assume a new home means new locks. Often it does not. Replacement makes sense when a lock is broken, badly worn, or you want to upgrade to a smart lock or a higher security grade. But if your existing locks work fine and just need new keys, rekeying gives you the same security outcome for less money and less disruption. At D Express Locksmith, we lead with rekeying first — it is usually the smarter call.

Here is a quick side-by-side:

| Factor | Rekeying | Full replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old keys stop working | Yes | Yes |
| Cost | Lower — reuses your hardware | Higher — new locks per door |
| Time on site | Fast, minutes per lock | Longer, hardware install |
| Keep matching door hardware | Yes | New finish/style |
| Best when | Locks work, keys unknown | Locks worn, broken, or upgrading |
| Match many doors to one key | Yes | Yes, with new locks |

One overlooked benefit: rekeying lets us set several different locks to open with a single key. If your Ambler home currently needs three different keys for the front, back, and garage, we can rekey all of them to one key on the same visit.

## How the rekeying visit works

The process is straightforward, and because we are fully mobile, it happens at your home. Here is the typical step-by-step:

1. **We confirm the scope.** You tell us how many doors and what type of locks — standard deadbolts, knob locks, a smart lock, or a mix.
2. **We verify the property is yours.** For a rekey we confirm you have the right to change the locks, which protects every homeowner we serve.
3. **We rekey each cylinder on site.** Pins come out, new pins go in, and we cut fresh keys matched to the new configuration.
4. **We test every lock.** You watch the old key fail and the new key work, on each door.
5. **We hand you the new keys** and, if you like, set multiple locks to a single key.

Most single-family rekeys are done in one visit with no damage to your doors — damage-free work is a core part of how we operate. If we find a lock that is genuinely failing, we will tell you plainly and give you the option to replace just that one, rather than upselling you on the whole house.

## Don't forget the garage, mailbox, and smart locks

Front and back doors get all the attention, but new homeowners routinely miss the other access points. The attached garage entry door is often the least-secure way into a house, and it is easy to overlook because you usually come in through the garage bay. If it is keyed, rekey it.

Mailbox locks, storage rooms, and any padlocked gates or sheds are also fair game and can frequently be rekeyed too. And if your new home came with a smart lock, don't assume the digital side is secure just because it has a keypad — old codes and paired phones may still be active. We can help you factory-reset user codes, and if the smart lock also has a physical key override, we can rekey that cylinder so the mechanical backup matches your new setup.

Takeaway: make one master list of every lock and every code in the house, then clear or change all of them at once. Partial security is a false sense of security.
